\section{Project Vision}\label{sec:project_vision}

Digital transformation has reshaped how educational institutions support
teaching, communication, learning access, and academic follow-up beyond the
physical classroom. As institutions adopt broader digital practices, the
expected quality of educational environments has also changed. Learners,
teachers, and administrators increasingly require systems that support
continuity, accountability, and consistent academic coordination.

Within this context, EduVerse is envisioned as a governed digital academic
environment that can support institutional growth while preserving coherence
across educational activity. The vision is intentionally strategic rather than
technical: to provide a stable and adaptable direction for organizing academic
work in a way that remains institutionally manageable and educationally
meaningful. The specific conditions that justify this vision are explained in
the following section.

\section{Problem Statement}\label{sec:problem_statement}

Current digital education practice is often weakened by the fragmentation of
closely related workflows across separate systems. Communication, content
access, live interaction, assessment, and routine class follow-up may all be
available, but they are frequently distributed across disconnected tools. As a
result, students, teachers, and administrators must move repeatedly between
multiple interfaces, which weakens workflow continuity, reduces institutional
oversight, and lowers instructional and administrative efficiency.

The problem becomes more serious when institutions operate across multiple
organizations, user roles, and access conditions. Membership handling,
permission control, class participation, and responsibility boundaries become
harder to manage when educational activity is scattered across unrelated
services. In such settings, weak coordination does not only inconvenience
users; it also limits accountability and makes reliable academic governance
more difficult.

A further challenge concerns device suitability. Rich authoring,
administration, and class-management tasks are better handled in a full web
environment, whereas frequent day-to-day actions such as checking classes,
following notifications, reviewing assignments, opening materials, joining
sessions, and reading messages should also remain practical on mobile devices.
This creates a concrete design problem for educational systems that must
support both depth of workflow and accessibility of use.

\section{Project Motivations}\label{sec:project_motivations}

EduVerse was worth building because it addresses a realistic educational
setting in which institutions need clearer digital coordination, teachers need
more dependable class workspaces, and students benefit from more consistent
access to academic activity. The project therefore has value beyond concept
development: it responds to operational needs that affect governance,
instruction, and day-to-day learning use.

The project was also a strong graduation topic because it allowed the team to
develop a practical educational system with relevance to contemporary digital
learning environments. It provided a meaningful opportunity to connect academic
software engineering work with an application domain that is socially useful,
operationally demanding, and suitable for future extension.

\section{Strategic Objectives}\label{sec:strategic_objectives}

The strategic objectives of EduVerse translate the project vision into clear
institutional and functional targets. They define what the system is intended
to support, govern, and extend within its educational context.

The main strategic objectives of the project are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item To provide a multi-organization educational management environment that
    can serve distinct institutions or organizational units within one system.
  \item To support multi-organization operation and role-based access for
    organization administrators, teachers, and students, including users with
    responsibilities across more than one organizational context.
  \item To organize classes, materials, communication, assignments,
    examinations, Results, and preserved academic history within a coherent
    class-centered workflow.
  \item To enable live sessions, AI Agent assistance, and the integrated
    coding workspace as built-in academic tools rather than external add-ons.
  \item To provide a primary web platform for full institutional and
    instructional workflows while extending selected high-frequency tasks
    through an MVP mobile companion application.
  \item To maintain controlled governance through configurable permissions,
    feature availability, teacher management controls, and visibility rules
    suited to different organizations and classes.
  \item To preserve Past Terms, academic records, and controlled assessment
    processes in a way that supports continuity and academic accountability.
  \item To extend the platform through controlled configuration and future
    service growth without compromising institutional structure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Educational and Professional Goals for the Team}\label{sec:team_goals}

For the project team, EduVerse serves as a full-cycle software engineering
exercise grounded in a realistic domain. It requires requirements analysis,
scope definition, workflow modeling, system design, implementation, service
integration, testing, and technical documentation within one sustained
development effort.

It also strengthens professional engineering practice. The project gives the
team experience in problem decomposition, collaborative development,
implementation trade-offs, verification, and disciplined reporting. In this
sense, its value for the team lies not only in producing software, but also in
practicing the standards of teamwork and technical responsibility expected in
professional software projects.

\section{Project Scope and Boundaries}\label{sec:project_scope}

The scope of EduVerse in this dissertation is defined around the implemented web
platform and the existing mobile MVP companion application. Chapter 1 is
limited to introducing the problem context, strategic direction, main user
groups, system boundaries, and general dissertation structure. Detailed
requirements, architecture, database design, implementation decisions, and
testing evidence are reserved for later chapters.

\subsection{System Boundaries and User Roles}\label{subsec:system_boundaries_roles}

EduVerse operates within a multi-organization educational context and therefore
is governed through three principal user roles: organization administrator,
teacher, and student. Organization administrators oversee membership
management, permission control, class oversight, feature configuration, and
broader organizational governance. Teachers manage instructional activities
within the permissions defined by organization settings, while students
participate in the academic spaces made available to them for learning and
follow-up.

A user may belong to multiple organizations, and the same individual may hold
more than one active role, with the interface and permissions resolved
according to the currently selected organization and role. Where enabled, the
system can also support controlled public access so that materials or
class-facing resources may be shared without removing governance from the
organization itself.

These role distinctions define important system boundaries. EduVerse is not a
generic social platform in which all users have the same functional position.
Instead, access is governed by organizational membership, class context, and
academic responsibility. This role-aware structure supports accountability,
controlled visibility, and more reliable institutional management.

\subsection{Functional Scope}\label{subsec:functional_scope}

Within its current implemented scope, EduVerse includes authentication,
organization access and membership, class management, materials,
communication, assignments, exams, results and academic history,
notifications, live sessions, an AI Agent, and an integrated coding
workspace. Together, these areas define the platform as an environment for
both academic management and active instructional workflows rather than as a
basic content portal. Within this scope, classes can be visible across the
organization, require term and stage definition, and move into Past Terms
while preserving academic history. Class feature controls, teacher management
abilities, student linkage to previous terms, and Results visibility are also
governed through organization and class settings.

The class workspace is also flexible enough to represent more than a
traditional course. The same class model can support focused academic uses,
including a digital library class organized primarily around sharing books and
learning materials.

At the same time, Chapter 1 remains introductory in nature. It does not attempt
to document detailed database structures, API behavior, or low-level internal
logic. Those aspects are part of the later analytical and technical chapters
and lie outside the present scope.

\subsection{Web and Mobile Client Scope}\label{subsec:web_mobile_client_scope}

The web application is the primary and most complete EduVerse client. It
supports the full institutional and instructional workflow and therefore
defines the main operational environment of the system.

The mobile application has a more focused scope. It is an MVP mobile companion
application intended to extend access to selected workflows such as
authentication, dashboard access, classes, assignments, notifications, chat,
materials, and live sessions. It is connected to the same broader platform
environment, but it is not intended to replace the web application or claim
full feature parity with it.

\subsection{Distinctive Features and Extensible Design}\label{subsec:competitive_features_innovation}

EduVerse is distinguished by its governed multi-organization structure and by
the way academic workflows are coordinated within that structure. Organization
administration, instructional management, and student participation are kept
within the same institutional model rather than being treated as unrelated
system layers.

The platform is also distinctive in treating the AI Agent and the integrated
coding workspace as built-in academic tools. Alongside these capabilities, the
system is designed for controlled extension: supported features may be
enabled, restricted, or expanded according to organizational and class needs,
while assessment practices and academic visibility remain subject to governed
rules.

At a high level, the platform follows a separated-service approach so that
major categories of academic data, learning resources, and real-time
interaction can evolve without collapsing into one tightly coupled service
layer. The detailed technical realization of this approach is discussed in the
later design and implementation chapters.
